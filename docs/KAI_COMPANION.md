# KAI desktop companion

Choose **Launch KAI** in the app's left sidebar. The main window moves out of the way and the same Core, wallet and earning node keep running. KAI appears above your desktop as a small animated robot. The tray menu also offers **Launch KAI companion** and **Hide KAI companion**.

- Click KAI or the chat bubble to open the conversation panel. Drag the robot to move him; his position is remembered and kept within a connected display's usable area.
- Type a question and press Enter. Shift+Enter adds a line. Replies stream from the selected model. KAI starts with the model selected in the main app and offers other downloaded models in the Brain menu.
- Tap the microphone, wait for the brief microphone-ready check, and speak without a wake phrase. A brief pause sends the question; a second tap can finish it sooner. Voice works with the chat closed. On Windows, the first use offers the existing local speech-engine download (about 156 MB). Audio is transcribed on this computer. If there is already a typed draft, voice is appended to that draft for review before sending.
- Voice mode turns spoken replies on. The footer's **Voice replies** button toggles playback. Open **Voice & listening** to download the local Kokoro neural voice model once (93 MB), choose Heart, Bella, Puck or Emma, and preview a greeting. Heart is the default, including a one-time upgrade from older computer-voice selections. Its download is also offered beside the compact robot. There is no silent OS fallback if the natural voice is missing or fails. Named local computer voices remain an explicit choice. Stop interrupts generation or playback. Escape cancels a recording or response before collapsing the panel.
- Conversations are saved in the existing app history with a **KAI** title. The plus button starts a new conversation without deleting the old one.
- Minimize the conversation to leave the robot on the desktop. **Open full app** returns to the normal app. The robot's three-dot menu offers a greeting, an animation toggle, and hiding to the tray.

KAI floats, blinks, looks around and rests when idle. Listening, thinking, speaking, greeting and dragging each have their own articulated animation. System reduced-motion preferences and the animation toggle are respected. The vector rig uses no extra AI model, WebGL engine or video decoder. Hiding the companion pauses its animation and stops microphone recording and voice playback.

## Hey KAI and desktop actions

Enable **Hey KAI** in the chat toolbar or waveform button in the dock. This is session-only opt-in: launching or reopening KAI does not automatically acquire the microphone. Say “Hey KAI” with your question, or say the name and then ask. The mic button starts a conversation directly. Both use one local AudioWorklet capture path with echo cancellation and noise suppression. Automatic gain is disabled to avoid amplifying background hiss; the detector calibrates the room level for the first 600 ms. The microphone indicator remains on throughout listening, transcription and replies. Where supported, all-system echo cancellation is requested to cover local speech output.

After an accepted question or wake phrase, further questions do not need the name. The conversation stays active throughout replies and for 60 seconds afterwards; the status pill reads **Your turn · mic on**. “That’s all,” “Thanks KAI,” or “Go to sleep” returns to wake-only listening. “Stop listening,” the waveform Off button, hiding, returning to the full app, disconnection and quitting release the device. Collapse keeps listening. The chat opens only by explicit interaction and each launch starts compact. Typed drafts receive voice text for review rather than being submitted automatically.

Local voice activity detection uses an adaptive noise floor, 300 ms pre-roll, separate start/end thresholds, rejection of 1.5 seconds of nearly constant hum, 180 ms minimum voice activity and 500 ms silence endpoint. Speech segments are bounded at 20 seconds. Capture continues while one Whisper transcription is running, with at most two queued segments; an overflow reports that a question should be repeated. A hung transcription is aborted after 30 seconds. Transient recognition errors keep the existing microphone stream open; three consecutive failures stop it once with an explicit error, without automatically restarting it. A question arriving while the wake phrase is being transcribed is retained. Ambient speech outside an active conversation is discarded before chat/history. Audio remains in bounded memory and temporary local transcription files are removed after processing. This still uses local Whisper after a pause, rather than an instant dedicated keyword engine.

During an active conversation, speech onset holds audible playback immediately, including blocking the next prepared phrase. Background candidates cannot hold a reply while its first phrase is still being synthesized. Each playback hold belongs to one capture segment: a newer sound cannot steal the hold or prevent an earlier rejected segment from releasing it. An unconfirmed hold lasts at most eight seconds; after timeout or two rejected holds in a reply, recognition continues but no more speculative pauses occur in that reply. Confirmed words still interrupt normally. AEC and comparison with recently spoken words reject residual speaker echo. A rejected/empty utterance resumes the same audio position even if another candidate is still being captured. Output that begins midway through capture is included in echo rejection. A confirmed user utterance cancels generation, synthesis and playback; the interrupted turn is saved before the new question starts. Partial assistant text and the original user question remain context, including an interruption before any answer text arrives. Recognition and synthesis latency remain hardware-dependent; real speaker/microphone placement can affect echo rejection.

Ask “bring up my Pictures folder” or “open my Downloads.” The complete, direct user command is matched before sending anything to the model. Supported folders are Pictures (also Photos), Documents, Downloads, Desktop, Music, Videos and Home. Main-process IPC validates the originating mascot main frame, accepts only the fixed folder names, resolves each through Electron app.getPath, and shows a native confirmation with Cancel selected by default. Approval applies once. Only then does shell.openPath open the OS folder. Stop or hiding invalidates a pending action. No renderer-supplied filesystem paths, URLs, executable names or shell commands are accepted. Results come from actual execution and are saved in the same chat history. This grants no reading, uploading, modifying, deleting, app launching or screen access.

## Local speech output

The sentence queue begins during SSE generation, hides code/reasoning from spoken output, and pre-generates one phrase while another plays. Stop cancels the stream, pending synthesis and playback. Kokoro's Apache-2.0 model is downloaded from an immutable Hugging Face revision with SHA-256 verification for all four model/tokenizer files; voice embeddings ship in the locked kokoro-js package. Inference disables remote model lookup, uses a separate Electron utility process (a worker thread in standalone Core) with two CPU threads, and unloads after two idle minutes. If native initialization, inference or the worker fails, the same pending request is retried once in a fresh process using ONNX WebAssembly with one CPU thread. The selected compatibility runtime is retained through idle unloads for the session. It uses the identical cached model and bundled voice embeddings; no cloud or OS voice is substituted. Compatibility inference can be slower. Cancellation and shutdown span both attempts. No model download occurs until **Get natural voice** or **Get natural voices** is clicked. Natural prosody uses the engine’s normal speed, without pitch shifting. If both engines fail, the setup card replaces progress with a retryable error, and downloaded files are retained and verified on retry. Model presence alone does not count as available after a failure. A failed engine or playback leaves the text available; users can select a computer voice. Voice input and speech output use separate optional models. First voice use may take a moment to warm up.

Upstream sources: [Kokoro JavaScript implementation and voices](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/tree/main/kokoro.js), [ONNX model](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX), [Electron shell](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/shell).

Windows has the pinned local voice engine. On platforms without that engine, typing remains available; spoken replies support the optional local neural engine or installed local system voices. Chat routing retains the app's existing privacy/network policy. Choosing a network model or using the app's Local-First fallback can send prompts to the configured network, just as in the main chat.

## Verification

The normal suite checks context budgeting, streaming boundaries and errors, model selection, window positioning, and IPC sender validation. The browser test exercises streaming chat, history, stop, real browser audio capture with a simulated microphone, local WAV submission, speech playback, microphone cleanup and motion controls. Test CI saves welcome and conversation screenshots.

Windows additionally launches the real companion controller and preload in Electron against a temporary fixture. It checks the transparent, always-on-top sandboxed window, launch/return behavior, streaming chat, compact/expanded bounds, and reusing one mascot window. The existing shared-live-profile and OS-credential check remains mandatory. Packaged ASAR verification checks every companion runtime asset is included.

The voice revision adds deterministic tests for early speech and cancellation, quieter activity detection, ambient speech rejection, follow-up timeout, capture during ASR, echo rejection, playback hold/resume and per-request folder approvals. Browser CI verifies compact natural-voice setup, real AudioWorklet capture with controlled audio, automatic endpointing, follow-ups, WAV playback, interruption before transcription finishes, stream cancellation with retained context, microphone lifetime and history. Its ASR results are controlled fixtures; actual hardware voice recognition is not simulated as a real speech-engine result. The isolated WASM worker redirects only Transformers’ eager native ONNX/common/image imports through Node’s synchronous module hooks (Node 22.15+; shipped Electron is newer). The ONNX Web runtime is pinned, its WASM files are unpacked beside its loader, and both Kokoro and Transformers share its Tensor class. Changes to these dependencies require real inference verification.

Windows checks the real preload action boundary and runs all four natural voices from the packaged ASAR with a temporary profile.

Windows CI also synthesizes wake-phrase fixtures with Heart and Puck, then runs them through the real pinned Whisper engine and KAI’s activity detector. It checks a quieter wake phrase and a follow-up without the name, over a synthetic steady background hum. Deterministic and browser regression tests cover successive false interruptions, slow/hung recognition, transient errors, stationary noise and a stable microphone stream. These checks do not measure recognition in the owner’s room or with their microphone.

Windows packaging also temporarily renames only the built app’s native speech addon and synthesizes all four voices from the ASAR under Electron, asserting automatic WASM recovery. The addon is restored in a finally block before installer creation; no system DLLs are modified. Final packaged native synthesis is checked separately. Browser CI verifies failed setup, clear error copy, and successful retry while keeping the mascot compact.
