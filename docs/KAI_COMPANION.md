# KAI desktop companion

Choose **Launch KAI** in the app's left sidebar. The main window moves out of the way and the same Core, wallet and earning node keep running. KAI appears above your desktop as a small animated robot. The tray menu also offers **Launch KAI companion** and **Hide KAI companion**.

- Click KAI or the chat bubble to open the conversation panel. Drag the robot to move him; his position is remembered and kept within a connected display's usable area.
- Type a question and press Enter. Shift+Enter adds a line. Replies stream from the selected model. KAI starts with the model selected in the main app and offers other downloaded models in the Brain menu.
- Tap the microphone, wait for the brief microphone-ready check, and speak without a wake phrase. A brief pause sends the question; a second tap can finish it sooner. Voice works with the chat closed. On Windows, the first use offers the existing local speech-engine download (about 156 MB). Audio is transcribed on this computer. If there is already a typed draft, voice is appended to that draft for review before sending.
- Voice mode turns spoken replies on. The footer's **Voice replies** button toggles playback. Open **Voice & listening** to download the local Kokoro neural voice model once (93 MB), choose Heart, Bella, Puck or Emma, and preview a greeting. Heart is the default, including a one-time upgrade from older computer-voice selections. Its download is also offered beside the compact robot. There is no silent OS fallback if the natural voice is missing or fails. Named local computer voices remain an explicit choice. **Voice character** defaults to **KAI · deeper, softly robotic**; **Natural · original sound** retains the original voice. Stop interrupts generation or playback. Escape cancels a recording or response before collapsing the panel.
- Conversations are saved in the existing app history with a **KAI** title. The plus button starts a new conversation without deleting the old one.
- Minimize the conversation to leave the robot on the desktop. **Open full app** returns to the normal app. The robot's three-dot menu offers a greeting, an animation toggle, and hiding to the tray.

KAI floats, blinks, looks around and rests when idle. Listening, thinking, speaking, greeting and dragging each have their own articulated animation. System reduced-motion preferences and the animation toggle are respected. The vector rig uses no extra AI model, WebGL engine or video decoder. Hiding the companion pauses its animation and stops microphone recording and voice playback.

## App capabilities

Desktop KAI shares the main chat's unified tool registry. There is no separate agent mode to enable: it can search/read the web when app privacy allows, use connected MCP tools and existing memory/workspace/email/calendar tools, and inspect the running app. It fetches current facts when asked, rather than putting the wallet or documents into every prompt. Tool results and accepted questions follow the selected model and existing privacy/routing/budget rules. Microphone audio remains local.

`app_read` exposes model catalogs and progress, KAI earnings, formatted wallet balances, node health and recent KOIN rewards, documents/chats, tasks, connections, account status, voice engines and feature switches. KAI pending earnings are the open epoch, not a daily historical total; blockchain rewards are KOIN, not KAI. The scheduler's balance cache can be up to 30 seconds old. Offline, unavailable, stale and syncing results must not be described as zero or current success. Passwords, backup keys, API secrets and sign-in codes are excluded.

`app_capabilities` discovers the supported reads/actions and gives exact action details on request. `app_action` adapts a fixed set of model, earning, node, document, schedule and settings controls to the existing Core routes. It does not accept arbitrary HTTP paths, RPC channels or extra arguments. Core enforces confirmation and privacy again; native approval shows the exact arguments with Cancel selected by default. The mic pauses processing during review. Cancel/Stop/hide invalidate a pending approval. Completed actions are not automatically retried, and an accepted background job may continue after its reply is stopped. Interrupted requests retain a receipt or an explicit unverified status in chat.

`app_open` can open every existing app section while leaving the mascot available. Direct requests such as “open up the application” do not need a model. Wallet signing, passwords, restore/backup/removal, deposits, bridges/swaps, reward automation, Docker/WSL setup, data moves, API credentials, account sign-in and host coding sessions open their existing screens rather than entering secrets or replacing established approvals with chat. Hidden feature sections lead to their Settings switch. The **Open full app** button still returns fully to the app and turns the mascot mic off.

The companion reuses the shared agent JSON parser and tool-name aliases, with up to six planning steps, a bounded prompt, a visible tool trail and clickable web sources. Simple read questions skip unnecessary planning. Final replies continue streaming into the existing sentence audio queue. Tool/web/document results are untrusted reference data in a separate user-role message; they are never system instructions or authorization. Weather needs a user-provided city and current sources, not an inferred device location. Tool selection and interpretation depend on the selected model.

## Hey KAI and desktop actions

Enable **Hey KAI** in the chat toolbar or waveform button in the dock. This is session-only opt-in: launching or reopening KAI does not automatically acquire the microphone. Say “Hey KAI” with your question, or say the name and then ask. The mic button starts a conversation directly. Both use one local AudioWorklet capture path with echo cancellation and noise suppression. Automatic gain is disabled to avoid amplifying background hiss; the detector calibrates the room level for the first 600 ms. The microphone indicator remains on throughout listening, transcription and replies. Where supported, all-system echo cancellation is requested to cover local speech output.

After an accepted question or wake phrase, further questions do not need the name. The conversation stays active throughout replies and for 60 seconds afterwards; the status pill reads **Your turn · mic on**. “That’s all,” “Thanks KAI,” or “Go to sleep” returns to wake-only listening. “Stop listening,” the waveform Off button, hiding, returning to the full app, disconnection and quitting release the device. Collapse keeps listening. The chat opens only by explicit interaction and each launch starts compact. Typed drafts receive voice text for review rather than being submitted automatically.

Local voice activity detection uses an adaptive noise floor, 300 ms pre-roll, separate start/end thresholds, rejection of 1.5 seconds of nearly constant hum, a selectable minimum voice activity of 180 ms (Quiet room), 240 ms (Everyday room) or 300 ms (TV nearby), and a 500 ms silence endpoint. Speech segments are bounded at 20 seconds. Capture continues while one Whisper transcription is running, with at most two queued segments; an overflow reports that a question should be repeated. A hung transcription is aborted after 30 seconds. Transient recognition errors keep the existing microphone stream open; three consecutive failures stop it once with an explicit error, without automatically restarting it. A question arriving while the wake phrase is being transcribed is retained. Ambient speech outside an active conversation is discarded before chat/history. Audio remains in bounded memory and temporary local transcription files are removed after processing. This still uses local Whisper after a pause, rather than an instant dedicated keyword engine.

**Voice & listening** defaults to **TV nearby**, with a .018 RMS onset floor and 2.5× adaptive room floor. Everyday room uses .012/2.2×; Quiet room uses .004/1.8×. A live meter shows whether sound is above the current onset threshold without storing audio. Changing these preferences resets pending candidates, not the microphone or conversation. These are loudness filters, not speaker identification. Quiet changing dialogue is rejected more often; loud dialogue can still become an ordinary follow-up after a reply.

**Say “KAI” to interrupt a reply.** This is now required in the companion, including profiles that previously turned the name guard off. “KAI” alone stops the reply and leaves the conversation open; “KAI” followed by a question stops the old answer and starts the new one. “Hey KAI” remains valid. Ordinary speech cannot pause or replace a reply. A bare name is accepted only during a reply, and must be a complete name token at the start of the utterance, never a substring of “okay,” “kayak,” or a mention later in a sentence. Audio captured during a reply retains its guard if transcription completes later. A question captured just after a separate recognized cue is retained for that same interrupted reply, without allowing an old cue to authorize interruptions of subsequent replies. During a guarded reply the minimum activity duration is 120 ms to retain the short name; the selected room loudness threshold remains in force.

After KAI finishes, ordinary follow-up speech remains active for 60 seconds. The mic button is an immediate manual interruption. AEC and comparison with recently spoken text reject residual speaker echo. Stop and recognized interruptions cancel generation, pending sentence assembly and playback. The interrupted turn is saved before the next prompt; partial assistant text and the original question remain context. One microphone stream stays open through recognition, replies and follow-ups. Recognition still uses local Whisper after a pause, so the name cue is not an instantaneous keyword detector.

Ask “bring up my Pictures folder” or “open my Downloads.” The complete, direct user command is matched before sending anything to the model. Supported folders are Pictures (also Photos), Documents, Downloads, Desktop, Music, Videos and Home. Main-process IPC validates the originating mascot main frame, accepts only the fixed folder names, resolves each through Electron app.getPath, and shows a native confirmation with Cancel selected by default. Approval applies once. Only then does shell.openPath open the OS folder. Stop or hiding invalidates a pending action. No renderer-supplied filesystem paths, URLs, executable names or shell commands are accepted. Results come from actual execution and are saved in the same chat history. This grants no reading, uploading, modifying, deleting, app launching or screen access.

## Local speech output

The sentence queue begins during SSE generation and excludes code, reasoning and raw links. It no longer emits short clauses or word-count fragments from ordinary sentences. A complete sentence is one playback unit, bounded at 1,200 characters for pathological output. Internally, long sentences use sequential synthesis requests of at most 240 characters, split at clause or word boundaries. All resulting PCM WAVs are validated and joined before playback. Excess silence between internal chunks is trimmed to about 80 ms; natural opening and closing silence remain. Cancelling assembly checks the abort signal between each chunk and never starts a later chunk after Stop.

Natural playback normally buffers two completed sentences before starting, then pre-generates up to two while the current sentence plays. A one-sentence reply starts once its stream ends and audio is complete; if the model stalls before emitting another sentence, a complete first sentence can start after a bounded 1.8-second buffer wait. The buffer does not run concurrent neural inferences. This favors coherent sentences over the previous short-fragment startup. It cannot guarantee continuous speech when the computer synthesizes more slowly than playback: waiting can still occur between sentences, and initial startup may be longer. The status reads **Getting my reply ready…** during preparation. Actual audio `playing` and OS utterance `start`/`resume` events drive the speaking animation; pause, waiting, ending, errors and Stop close the mouth. Late playback events cannot revive stopped animation.

The **KAI** character applies a local PCM treatment to the same chosen voice: resampling at a 0.9 ratio lowers pitch about 1.8 semitones and makes playback about 11% longer, with a light 55 Hz amplitude texture (0.8–1.0 gain) and 5 ms edge fades. This needs no inference, model, account, new audio process or download. It preserves silence and does not amplify above the input peak. **Natural** leaves the original voice unprocessed. Named OS voices use their own pitch/rate controls for the deeper setting; the electronic texture applies to the neural voices.

When an installed natural voice is selected, starting listening or sending a question requests `/core/speech/warm` to load the existing engine while the user speaks or the chat model works. This never downloads a model or generates audible output. Concurrent warm-up callers share one request; a real phrase waits for it and remains cancellable. Cancelling a waiting phrase does not cancel the shared model load or allow that phrase to start later. The normal idle unload still applies. Stop cancels the stream, pending synthesis and playback. Kokoro's Apache-2.0 model is downloaded from an immutable Hugging Face revision with SHA-256 verification for all four model/tokenizer files; voice embeddings ship in the locked kokoro-js package. Inference disables remote model lookup, uses a separate Electron utility process (a worker thread in standalone Core) with two CPU threads, and unloads after two idle minutes. If native initialization, inference or the worker fails, the same pending request is retried once in a fresh process using ONNX WebAssembly with one CPU thread. The selected compatibility runtime is retained through idle unloads for the session. It uses the identical cached model and bundled voice embeddings; no cloud or OS voice is substituted. Compatibility inference can be slower. Cancellation and shutdown span both attempts. No model download occurs until **Get natural voice** or **Get natural voices** is clicked. Neural inference uses normal speed; the selected voice character is applied afterwards to the assembled audio. If both engines fail, the setup card replaces progress with a retryable error, and downloaded files are retained and verified on retry. Model presence alone does not count as available after a failure. A failed engine or playback leaves the text available; users can select a computer voice. Voice input and speech output use separate optional models. First voice use may take a moment to warm up.

Upstream sources: [Kokoro JavaScript implementation and voices](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/tree/main/kokoro.js), [ONNX model](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX), [Electron shell](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/shell).

Windows has the pinned local voice engine. On platforms without that engine, typing remains available; spoken replies support the optional local neural engine or installed local system voices. Chat routing retains the app's existing privacy/network policy. Choosing a network model or using the app's Local-First fallback can send prompts to the configured network, just as in the main chat.

## Verification

The normal suite checks context budgeting, streaming boundaries and errors, model selection, window positioning, and IPC sender validation. The browser test exercises streaming chat, history, stop, real browser audio capture with a simulated microphone, local WAV submission, speech playback, microphone cleanup and motion controls. Test CI saves welcome and conversation screenshots.

Windows additionally launches the real companion controller and preload in Electron against a temporary fixture. It checks the transparent, always-on-top sandboxed window, launch/return behavior, streaming chat, compact/expanded bounds, and reusing one mascot window. The existing shared-live-profile and OS-credential check remains mandatory. Packaged ASAR verification checks every companion runtime asset is included.

The voice revision adds deterministic tests for early speech and cancellation, quieter activity detection, ambient speech rejection, follow-up timeout, capture during ASR, echo rejection, playback hold/resume and per-request folder approvals. Browser CI verifies compact natural-voice setup, real AudioWorklet capture with controlled audio, automatic endpointing, follow-ups, WAV playback, interruption before transcription finishes, stream cancellation with retained context, microphone lifetime and history. Its ASR results are controlled fixtures; actual hardware voice recognition is not simulated as a real speech-engine result. The isolated WASM worker redirects only Transformers’ eager native ONNX/common/image imports through Node’s synchronous module hooks (Node 22.15+; shipped Electron is newer). The ONNX Web runtime is pinned, its WASM files are unpacked beside its loader, and both Kokoro and Transformers share its Tensor class. Changes to these dependencies require real inference verification.

Windows checks the real preload action boundary and runs all four natural voices from the packaged ASAR with a temporary profile.

Windows CI also synthesizes wake-phrase fixtures with Heart and Puck, then runs them through the real pinned Whisper engine and KAI’s activity detector. It checks a quieter wake phrase and a follow-up without the name, over a synthetic steady background hum. Deterministic and browser regression tests cover successive false interruptions, slow/hung recognition, transient errors, stationary noise and a stable microphone stream. These checks do not measure recognition in the owner’s room or with their microphone.

Windows packaging also temporarily renames only the built app’s native speech addon and synthesizes all four voices from the ASAR under Electron, asserting automatic WASM recovery. The addon is restored in a finally block before installer creation; no system DLLs are modified. Final packaged native synthesis is checked separately. Browser CI verifies failed setup, clear error copy, and successful retry while keeping the mascot compact.

The TV revision additionally tests changing low-level dialogue, stronger nearby speech, optional wake-guarded interruption including delayed recognition, warm-up sharing/cancellation, and early first clauses. Browser CI checks preparation versus actual playback animation, pause/Stop/late events, the listening settings, and guarded movie dialogue. Windows feeds real synthesized quiet dialogue through the TV detector, transcribes louder dialogue without accepting it as an interruption, and accepts a real wake phrase with the guard enabled. These fixtures do not establish voice identity or guarantee rejection of an equally loud television in a physical room.

The sentence revision adds tests for complete streamed sentences, sequential chunk assembly and cancellation, bounded initial buffering, PCM validation, pitch change, clipping/silence, and a brief KAI cue with a question captured during recognition. Browser CI checks name-only and inline KAI interruptions with retained context, follow-ups without the name, actual audio animation and persistent voice-character selection. Real Windows recognition exercises the isolated word “KAI” from Heart and Puck. Both native and forced-WASM packaged checks assemble a sentence longer than the inference limit and apply the robot character to real neural output.
